## Outcome

`scoreCard` stops being blind to WHEN a DoT's HP arrives. Today `statusPoints`
prices a DoT at its **printed lifetime HP ÷ 3** — a RUPTURE-fast BLEED that
dumps 30 HP in ~2 rounds and a POISON that ramps 30 HP over ~6 rounds price
**identically**. 36b weights each tick by the probability the fight is still
going when it lands, so a slow ramp whose big ticks fall past the ~4-round
death clock is no longer **overpaid** at its printed lifetime, while a
front-loaded DoT keeps ~full value.

This is a **rank-honesty / model-fidelity** fix (the lint's job), the tempo
parallel of 36a: 36a made the model see alt-win currencies that priced at ~0;
36b makes it see **tempo** — the axis the erosion ladder and the fix-card test
proved is the one that converts price → wins. It does **not** itself climb the
late wall (FINDINGS rec #4: that is an enemy/survivability problem the price
lever can't move — the erosion ladder crawls late 3%→9% across 4× price). Its
payoff is **downstream design pressure**: when the mid/late decks get built,
`/deck-tuning` gets a budget that rewards front-loaded cards and stops
overpaying slow ramps.

## The mechanism

`statusPoints` now prices DoTs via a new **tempo-weighted** walk. Each tick
that lands in combat round `r` is worth `DOT_TEMPO_SURVIVAL ^ (r-1)` of its
printed HP:

- **Global horizon `DOT_TEMPO_SURVIVAL = 0.75`.** Mean fight length
  `1/(1-p) = 4.0` rounds, self-consistent with the measured `avgRoundsAll`
  ≈ 4.06 and the existing `NO_CALENDAR_PRICING_ROUNDS = 4`.
- **Geometric (memoryless)** — reads literally as "probability the fight is
  still going in round r," matching the FINDINGS mechanism (a ~constant
  per-round death hazard: enemy output vs fixed HP, so `avgRoundsAll` holds
  flat against card price).
- **One-sided discount** (weight ≤ 1, = 1 in round 1). No >1 front-load
  *bonus* — a bonus would push finishers (`resonance-detonation` 18.13,
  `the-overtake` 18.35) through the rank-5 ceiling of 19. The relative gap
  opens by ceasing to OVERPAY slow, not by subsidizing fast.

## Decisions made upfront — DO NOT ASK

1. **GLOBAL horizon, NOT per-stage.** The row says "discount long-ramp DoTs by
   expected fight length *per stage*." A per-stage horizon injects
   enemy-derived state (`avgRoundsAll` per stage) into a function whose whole
   job is stage-independence — it would break the exact discipline 36a
   preserved (36a kept the CONCEDE capstone a literal `3` and deferred
   elite/boss floor scaling as "enemy-dependent → 36b"). FINDINGS rec #4
   forbids the per-stage reading directionally ("fix late via enemies, not
   price"). **Reinterpretation:** "per stage" = "calibrate the ONE global
   horizon to the measured global death-clock (~4)"; the late stage is simply
   where that clock bites hardest, but the clock is a global fact about enemy
   output. The tempo *signal* (front-loaded beats slow) is stage-invariant by
   nature.
2. **Geometric curve, p = 0.75.** Not a hard cutoff (brittle at the cliff) or
   a linear taper (implies a rising hazard with an arbitrary zero). Marked
   `// PLAYTEST-CALIBRATION`; FINDINGS rec #5 flags seeds=2 origin, so 0.75 is
   the principled starting point, not a proven optimum — `/deck-tuning` is the
   empirical court for a higher-seed confirmation.
3. **ARCHITECTURE: `dotLifetimeHp` stays PURE.** It mirrors the engine's
   detonation math (`getPendingDotTotal`) so the lint and the RUPTURE preview
   never diverge. The tempo weight lives in a separate `dotTempoWeightedHp`
   (both delegate to a shared `walkDotHp(…, weightFn)`); only `statusPoints`
   routes through the weighted version. This is what keeps the fallout small
   — the **printed-lifetime anchors** (poison=20, bleed=9/18, doom=14) stay
   verbatim green.
4. **Weight the HP, never the intensity.** `w(r)` multiplies the floored
   per-tick HP AFTER ramp/decay/growth; the BLEED washout early-return still
   tests the UNWEIGHTED intensity, so *when* a BLEED washes out is unchanged.
5. **The POISON double-hit is intended.** Ramp concentrates value at rounds
   3-4; the tempo discount bites rounds 3-4 — a ramping DoT pays the tax
   exactly where survival is least likely. That IS the FINDINGS mechanism, not
   over-punishment (penitent's slow akrasia ramp is the worst offender).
6. **Harness down-scaler clamp fixed** (`price-winrate.harness.ts`): the old
   `Math.round` made x0.5/x0.75 near-no-ops (round(0.75×2)=2 → identical to
   x1). Down-tiers now FLOOR so they actually shed intensity/duration;
   up-tiers keep round() so the published up-ladder is unchanged. Still coarse
   by integer-intensity construction.

## Lint fallout (computed; no card leaves its band)

Movers are exactly the ramping/round-clocked DoT carriers; BLEED cards are
tempo-immune. Library: `slippery-slope` 7.42→5.05 (was near its Doxa CEILING —
the reprice IMPROVES rank honesty), `sweet-poison`/`mounting-case` and the
short-poison peroration/oracle cards down 0.1–2.4, all in band. Sandbox
anchors: `tf-cinder` 4.5→3.81, `slag-runoff` 5.5→5.27, `grit-between-stones`
7.5→6.58, `in-medias-res` 7.07→6.73, `dying-echo` 6.42→6.08 — all in band.
`pact-of-akrasia` (the rank-5 floor card) does NOT move (self-bleed is
front-loaded). Monotonicity preserved (only DoT ranks drop). **No re-band.**
