Can you work backwards?

Yes. A deck idea first is exactly what a slice pitch is. One step changes: the keywords you imagine for the deck enter as codenamed mechanics with sample cards, not as ratified glossary rows. They get their real names after play. Ratifying keywords before cards is the step that sank Phase 29/30. Confidence 80.

Your question: start with a deck idea, then build keywords, then cards. Short answer: the deck-first part is supported by every comparator. The keywords-before-cards part is the known failure. Swap it for codenames and you keep the whole intent.

Why this works

  • Wizards hands off headline mechanics as codenames plus sample cards and names them late (§8 R7-02 naming).
  • A keyword is a count decision made after the cards exist (§8 R7-01 naming).
  • Roughly half of named mechanics die before print, so an early name is usually wasted (§8 R7-04 naming).
  • Mega Crit built each archetype as a card batch, then sculpted it from play (§8 R1-01).
  • Phase 29/30 fixed a 31-row keyword registry first, then fitted 70 cards to it (§0).

What changes if you insist on real keywords first

It still runs, with a cost. Each keyword ratified before play costs its 12 wiring steps up front, and about half are cut later. The §1 carrier rule (a keyword needs ≥3 kept cards) will then fail some of them at the kill session. Expect rework on roughly one keyword in two. Confidence 60.
