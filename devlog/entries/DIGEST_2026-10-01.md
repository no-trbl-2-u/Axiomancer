# 2026-10-01 — Combat sheds its rock-paper-scissors stance system

> The reset the last digest announced keeps moving, fast: twelve more
> build-plan phases shipped in the two days since. The old fishing
> village and the entire northern continent — forest, caverns, two
> cities, a river crossing — are now actually deleted, not just closed
> off behind a sealed door. Potions drop to three honest heals, and
> hazard-run rewards finally pay what their labels promise. Relic
> skills simplify to a single shield, and sparing a beaten foe through
> the Suppliant's Ring now genuinely works. The biggest backstage
> change: the rock-paper-scissors stance system — the pre-round stance
> choice, the enemy "tell," the three-way read that used to govern
> every exchange — is gone from combat entirely. Card colour and the
> momentum chain survive untouched. Along the way the designer also
> picked how cards come back once the reset finishes: one small family
> of lanes at a time, not the whole shelf at once.

## [mechanics] Combat sheds its rock-paper-scissors stance system

**What:** Every piece of the old stance system is gone: the pre-round
stance choice, the "yield or get punished" check at the end of a phase,
enemy stance tells and reveals, the card-detail "read" triplet that told
you whether a card's keyword would land harder or softer, and the
DISRUPT status that worked by denying a stance. A guard preview now
just shows a number plus the colour-match bonus — no reveal step, no
advantage/disadvantage chip to read beforehand.

**Why:** The designer's reset plan kept card colour and the momentum
chain but ruled the separate rock-paper-scissors stance layer out: it
was a second system riding alongside colour that the four-region,
three-monster core no longer needed carrying. Removing it now, before
the card-rebuild sessions start, means nobody designs a new card around
a mechanic that was already scheduled to disappear.

**Commits:** `2a727668` (stance removal, R7d), `1802385a` (stance
residue and copy sweep, R7d2)

**No capture:** no baseline screenshot was recaptured this window (only
a doc file under `screenshots/baseline/` changed), so the nightly shot
tool found nothing to diff for the removed stance chips and badges.

## [mechanics] Relics simplify to one shield, and sparing a foe finally works

**What:** Ten of the eleven relic skills ("signatures") now do the same
thing: raise a GUARD 5 shield for one flat Conviction cost. The
eleventh, the Suppliant's Ring ("The Open Hand"), is now the game's one
mercy action — used against a beaten-down Brine Hag, it opens a real
spare-or-exploit choice. Used against the other two monsters it refuses
and spends nothing, rather than silently doing nothing like the old
"Quarter" effect did.

**Why:** The old signature kit (reroll, scout, sustain, and other dice
tricks) was built for a card pool and monster roster the reset has
mostly deleted; keeping it would mean re-balancing eleven bespoke skills
against three monsters. Collapsing ten of them to one shared shield, and
making the eleventh an honest mercy action instead of a dead effect,
gives relics a floor to rebuild from in a later guided session instead
of carrying dead weight into the checkpoint release.

**Commits:** `9110c7be` (R4)

**No capture:** relics aren't part of the nightly Cards/Enemies/Effects
catalog tool, so there's no auto-generated before/after plate for a
relic description change; all eleven relics keep their names, and only
their one-line descriptions changed.

## [content] The old world's deleted places are actually gone, not just closed off

**What:** The fishing village (its map, NPCs, quests, and the "village
goodwill" shop-discount system) and the entire parked northern continent
(five maps, thirteen NPC trees, nine quests, seven shops, about 1,580
lines of map events) are now deleted from the game outright, not merely
sealed behind a closed door the way the last digest described. A save
that pointed at any of those places moves to the Lantern Deep on load.
One blacksmith ("Anvil") now sits near the exit of each of the four
surviving regions, replacing the village's smithy. Five unused
combat-arena images that belonged to those maps are deleted with them.

**Why:** The reset's roadmap always planned to delete the parked
continent outright once Act One could stand on its own; this window
reaches that point for both the fishing village (R3b/R3c) and the rest
of the parked world (R7e). Leaving the code and data in the tree after
sealing the doors would have meant every later reset step re-checking
itself against six maps nobody can reach — deleting it now lets the
remaining steps, and the eventual content rebuild, work from the real,
final map.

**Commits:** `25a6c5e7` (fishing village + goodwill, R3b), `b901c094`
(Act 1 hygiene, shillings-only currency, R3c), `1017c8d5` (the rest of
the parked world, R7e)

**No capture:** deleting a map leaves no "after" picture to pair with a
"before" one; the nightly plate-evidence tool found no added or
replaced plate to capture this window.

## [mechanics] Potions and hazard-run rewards become simple and honest

**What:** The item shelf drops to three healing potions (minor, normal,
greater); the other nineteen consumables are gone. Separately,
hazard-run rewards now pay exactly what their on-screen label says: two
flat shillings payouts replace three old rewards that used to promise
relics, a hoard, or a token that quietly set a flag nothing read. The
"sub-quest" system hazard runs used to offer is deleted end to end,
since one of its three reward kinds was that same dishonest token.

**Why:** Both changes close the same kind of gap — UI that promised
something the game didn't actually grant. Potions above "greater" had no
monster left strong enough to need them (Act One tops out around level
8), and the hazard rewards that said "relics" or "a hoard" were paying
nothing of the kind. The designer's rule for this reset is that a
reward either pays what it says or gets replaced, not relabeled.

**Commits:** `b2f91e00` (items reset, R5), `ab9e5378` (hazard rewards,
R6a), `f0a230f5` (hazard deck down to ten cards, R6b)

**No capture:** no baseline screen changed its pixels enough to trigger
the nightly shot tool (copy and tuning changed, not layout); the
catalog tool tracks player cards, monsters, and status effects, not
items or hazard cards.

## [mechanics] The engine's biggest cleanup yet: five ticks removing dead systems

**What:** Across five shipped ticks, the combat engine lost: every
card-keyword system no live card used (47 retired kinds); card synergy,
themes, and the old deck-draft/preset system (cards are now just
Attack, Skill, or Spell); the three "alternate win" systems —
persuasion ("Plea"), a threat-building meter ("Charge"), and the
surrender/concede endings (sparing a foe through the Suppliant's Ring
is now the only non-lethal ending); a damage-denial ladder ("Stagger")
that nothing fed anymore; and twenty-one data fields no card or monster
effect still printed. One status effect's internal tag changed along
the way (Creeping Doom's shared `DOOM` label became its own
`CREEPING_DOOM`), with no change to what it reads on screen.

**Why:** Each of these systems had already lost its last card, keyword,
or monster to an earlier reset step, leaving engine code with nothing
left to drive it — dead weight that made the codebase harder to read
and every later reset step slower to verify. Clearing it now, before
the card-rebuild sessions start, means nobody can accidentally
resurrect a retired mechanic by copying old code.

**Commits:** `b7f74606` (R7a), `56b0cba7` (R7b), `00277f40` (R7c),
`7a463a04` (R7c2), `c7d0ce6b` (R7c3)

**Evidence:** rule debuff_creeping_doom — reworded

## [infra] A same-day catch: an incomplete purge commit never reached players

**What:** The automated check that runs on every change caught a commit
that claimed to fully delete the fishing village but had actually only
removed 15 files without updating the code that still referenced them —
a map file still exported the deleted village, and other code still
called functions that no longer existed. The gate failed closed the
same tick the commit landed, before it could reach a release, and the
real, complete deletion shipped right after.

**Why:** The standing rule for a red build is "fix the root cause,
never patch around it" — worth naming because it shows that rule
holding even under this week's unusually large and fast-moving reset
work, not just on an ordinary day.

**Commits:** `817a090e` (the catch), `25a6c5e7` (the complete fix)

**No capture:** a CI gate catching a bad commit has no player-facing
screen to capture.

## [mechanics] The designer picks how cards come back: family by family

**What:** In an attended planning session, the designer picked the
process the upcoming card-rebuild sessions will follow: cards return
one "lane" at a time (not one card, and not the whole shelf at once),
lanes group into small families, and a relic will eventually name each
family. The first batch is three families of one lane each, with
"bridge" cards connecting lanes inside the same family.

**Why:** The designer's own stated reasoning: the last two times cards
were rebuilt from scratch, work started by defining keywords or writing
cards with no plan for how much to build before checking the result,
and the whole card shelf became the unit of work either way. Picking
"one lane, then a family, then move on" up front is meant to keep each
future session small enough to actually finish.

**Commits:** `260a0d9f` (D73)

**No capture:** a process decision for future sessions, not a change to
anything in the game today.
