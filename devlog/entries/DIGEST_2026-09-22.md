# 2026-09-22 — The map opens, and your cards tell the truth

> Three days, one theme: the game stops misleading you. Card details now print
> what the card actually does. Forty cards had been hiding or garbling a line,
> two of them hiding 70 and 90 damage. The map is no longer a single ladder:
> every place you have reached stays open, and only the ground you have already
> spent is closed. You now start with the Suppliant's Ring in your hand instead
> of finding it silently in a pocket. Rarity is readable without colour. Your
> whole deck has its own tab. Underneath that: a returning player no longer
> gets a blank screen, the fishing village gained three guaranteed fights
> before its king, new pilgrims start with a plain grey deck, three more regions
> fight on their own engraved ground, and this DevLog now has a public home
> at axiomancer-devlog.pages.dev. All 41 of the automated loop's ticks this
> window finished cleanly.

## [ui] Card details print what the card does

**What:** The panel you open by holding a card in a fight used to rebuild the
card's text on its own, from scratch, in the app. It skipped any clause it had
no badge for. Forty cards printed a paid line that disagreed with the card
itself: 25 lost a whole clause, and 15 printed a clause with its numbers
missing. The Vein Called In and The Last Assize hid 90 and 70 damage
respectively. The engine now writes each card's free and paid lines out as a
list of clauses, and the panel only formats that list. A test runs over every
card in the library and checks the panel's words, byte for byte, against the
engine's own rules text. The panel was also cleared out: flavour prose is gone
from combat (it moved to the new DECK tab), keyword definitions sit at the top
above the card, and the card's rarity rides the strip beside its name.

**Why:** The maintainer played the game and reported that card details
"sometimes appear not to match" the card, and called it the most important of
nine problems. The audit that followed found it was not "sometimes": a
second, hand-built copy of the rules text had drifted from the real one on 40
cards. A player choosing which card to pay for was being shown the wrong price
or the wrong payoff. The decluttering answers the same report's "the card
details are just too busy" and "keyword definitions should be at the top".

**Commits:** `872dd92`, `4fcb6f2` (PR #353)

**No capture:** The screen baselines this change touches did not move by the
2% the capture step needs. The fix is in the words, not the layout, so no

## [ui] Rarity you can read without colour

**What:** A card's rarity used to be a single coloured wax dot. That is
unreadable in greyscale, to a colourblind player, and to anyone never told
that purple means rare, which is everyone, because nothing in the game said
so. Card faces now carry a three-slot pip track, filled up to the card's band
and hollow past it, so the count is a shape. Large faces and the reward tiles
print the band's name outright. Small faces keep pips and hue and speak the
word to a screen reader. The track runs down the side rather than across,
because a row would have eaten a third of every fanned card's name.

**Why:** Owner report: "I don't see any way to immediately recognize the
card's rarity." The owner chose label, pips and frame together over any single
signal, so that no one channel carries the meaning alone.

**Commits:** `3637194` (PR #353)

**No capture:** No baseline screen moved past the capture threshold, so there
is no before/after image for this change.

## [ui] A DECK tab for your whole deck

**What:** A fifth tab, DECK, sits beside SATCHEL and lists every card in your
run deck. The list is grouped by type (spells cycle through the reshuffle;
oaths and hexes stand), then sorted by rank. It is the one place flavour text
now lives. Every printed line comes from the same call the combat panel makes,
so the two cannot disagree. A fifth label forced a measurement of the tab
bar: THE LEDGER was silently clipped on small phones (54pt of room, 55.8pt of
word). Letter-spacing was tightened until it fits at 320pt.

**Why:** There was no way to look at your own deck outside a fight. The owner
asked for one, and asked for flavour text to live there rather than in
combat, where it competes with the numbers you are deciding on.

**Commits:** `6acae87` (PR #353)

**No capture:** No baseline screen moved past the capture threshold, so there
is no before/after image for the new tab.

## [mechanics] The map branches, and you can walk back

**What:** The map used to be a ladder: once you moved on, everything behind
you was closed. Now the whole frontier you have explored stays open, and you
can travel across it freely. A node closes only once you have resolved it
(fought it, rested at it, taken its treasure). The chart draws three states
that differ in fill, size and shape, not just colour: spent, open and sealed.
The loud red cross is retired. It had been repeated until the sheet was
unreadable, and it had been marking nodes you had already answered as
"sealed", which is backwards. 69 new side paths are inked at half width so the
main road stays easy to find. The legend no longer runs under the compass,
and a RECENTRE control brings the camera back to you.

**Why:** Owner report: "The map is purely vertical so let's make branches
more like a tree... allow players to move freely around the map, only locking
nodes if the player already explored that node." The owner's ruling on
detail was that visited means spent, and everything else stays free.

**Commits:** `1da1693`, `4acab7d` (PR #353)

**No capture:** No baseline screen moved past the capture threshold, so there
is no before/after image of the map.

## [mechanics] The Suppliant's Ring is handed to you, and a relic is a decision

**What:** Every new pilgrim used to start with no items shown, the ring seeded
silently into the save. Now the first node hands it over through a new reward
screen. That screen shows the item, its signature skill and what it grants,
and offers two buttons: CONFIRM (into your satchel) or EQUIP (into your
satchel and onto you). If your accessory row is full, it names exactly which
item wearing this one would push out, so a swap is never blind. Backing out
of the screen still keeps the item. Every item grant in the game now goes
through one shared path.

**Why:** Owner report: "Each new player starts with no items. The first node
should give the player the Suppliant's Ring to start." The owner then asked
for the reward screen with those two buttons. The owner ruled that the screen
shows for any item carrying a signature or a stat grant, that a slot conflict
swaps the old item into the satchel, and that dismissing the screen counts as
CONFIRM.

**Commits:** `0450548`, `bba6a82`, `087245e` (PR #353)

**No capture:** The reward screen is new and has no committed baseline, so
there is no before/after image to show.

## [ui] Gathering does something you can see, and every toast is visible again

**What:** Stepping onto a gathering node used to look like nothing happened.
Now a card acknowledges what you found. The deeper cause turned out to be
app-wide: the small notice strip at the top of the screen had been painted
underneath the navigator the whole time. Every toast in the game was silent:
crossing into a region, the "accessory slots full" refusal, the cost of
fleeing. There was nothing to see and no error anywhere. The notice strip
now sits on top, and a test stops a style tidy-up from muting it again.

**Why:** Owner report: "The Gather node is now a no-op." It was not a no-op
underneath: the gain was real, but its only announcement was drawn under an
opaque screen.

**Commits:** `ab6e80b`, `80b7b22` (PR #353)

**No capture:** No baseline screen moved past the capture threshold.

## [mechanics] New pilgrims start with a grey deck

**What:** A fresh run now opens with ten plain grey cards: seven Grey Strike
and three Grey Ward. Any die, wild included, can power them, and they carry
no stance. The first three card rewards you take are drawn uniformly at
random. From the fourth onward, rewards lean toward the keywords already in
your deck, so a build starts to pull in its own direction.

**Why:** Phase 104. The starting deck used to pre-decide a build before the
player had made a single choice. A colourless opening deck lets the first
rewards set the direction, and the keyword pull then rewards the direction
you chose.

## [balance] The fishing village: three gates before the king

**What:** The grey deck lost every fight against the King of Revenge. He is
pinned at level 3, and his HIDE 3 turned the grey deck's 2 and 5 damage into
1 and 2. The fix leaves the grey cards as they were. Instead, HIDE is now
capped by a foe's level (none at levels 1 to 3), and only HIDE ramps this way.
The village map gained three single-lane gate nodes (Tide-Line, Salt Flats
and Breakwater Steps), so every route fights a grave-larva, a float-eye and a
chattering skull before the King. Measured over 60 runs per cell: after one
random reward, a fresh grey deck beats the King 93% of the time. With no
reward at all it is 0%.

**Why:** The maintainer ruled no card buff and no handicap on how many
rewards you get. A win with no reward taken should stay possible only through
near-perfect play. The gates guarantee every player the rewards the opening
was balanced around.

**Commits:** `65d4abe`, `36ba0e6`

## [mechanics] Summoners, and three more regions on their own ground

**What:** A new kind of foe fields bodies of its own. The first is The
Jeweled Tree, with two Brier Shoots. Each body bites for a flat amount every
threat phase. You clear one with a strike that costs Conviction but no die.
Clearing the brood is progress, never victory: waves are capped at two and
never respawn because you emptied the board. The telegraph had been showing
DENIED ("no damage lands") on a staggered summoner while its brood still
bit. It now prints the brood's share of the damage too. Separately, the
Northern City, the Connecting River and the Sweetheart's Village now fight
in front of their own Gustave Doré engravings, and so do The Caverns and The
Capital. That brings bespoke arenas from 1 of 7 regions to 6 of 7. The
fallback arena was a saturated pixel-art cityscape of untraced licence among
nine wood engravings. It has been retired for Doré's "The New Zealander"
(1873), the desolation plate below.

**Why:** Phases 101 to 103. A fight in a borrowed city looked like every
other fight. Summoning was the last enemy archetype still missing from the
filed gaps (`adjust-enemies`). Retiring the fallback plate removed the most-seen
art in the game whose right to be published could not be shown.
