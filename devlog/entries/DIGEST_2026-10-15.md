# 2026-10-15 — The combat screen gets a full redesign

> Two days of work land at once: every card now wears one consistent face,
> the fight screen's buttons and bars move into a single dock at the
> bottom, and the exploration map stops drawing underneath a live fight.
> Alongside that, three self-checking tools mentioned two entries ago went
> live and found real things — a card that broke its own point budget 18
> times over, a ring nobody can earn, and a newer deck that wins every
> test fight it's thrown into. Below is what changed and why.

## [ui] The combat screen gets a full redesign: one card face, one footer dock

**What:** Every card in the game now draws the same way: a small pill in
the corner for anything free to play, one-line names, the die cost next
to the paid cost, and no more rarity dots cluttering the art. Underneath
that, the whole bottom of the fight screen was rebuilt: your portrait,
your life total (now a bar that turns red under a quarter health), a
record of how each phase of the fight went, and the END button all live
together in one strip, instead of scattered medallions and corners. The
log moved into the player menu instead of sitting on the board.

**Why:** the mismatched card faces and scattered HUD pieces were called
out repeatedly in the game's own fresh-eyes testing passes, and T signed
off on the new layout from on-screen prototypes before it was built
(decisions D91 "one card face, everywhere" and D92 "the combat board
layout"). The goal was simply a fight screen that reads as one thing
instead of several.

**Commits:** `507bdf13` (card face), `00bd78cf` (footer dock), plus three
same-day polish fixes: `9d44ca5e` (a hand card's FREE pill stayed on
screen), `0879fb60` (the life-total column stopped running a screen's
width wide on desktop), `b761f948` (the END button stopped floating in
empty space on a wide window).

**No capture:** this window's automated before/after picture set has no
entry for the combat board yet, so no automated comparison could be
generated for this ledger. The change is real and live — it replaced
essentially the whole bottom half of the fight screen.

## [ui] The exploration map stops rendering underneath a live fight

**What:** The map screen used to keep drawing underneath the fight screen
the whole time you were in combat — the terrain, paths and markers just
sat there, hidden, the entire fight. Now the map unmounts the moment a
fight starts and remounts, centred on your position, the moment it ends.

**Why:** T reported this as a real jank problem on the installed app
("I no longer want the exploration map rendering underneath combat") and
asked for it immediately, ahead of everything else queued. Drawing a map
nobody can see for the whole length of every fight was pure wasted work
for the device.

**Commits:** `96643425` (phase PF4)

**No capture:** a performance fix to what un-renders behind the screen
you already see; there is nothing to show a before/after of.

## [mechanics] A second copy of a Global card can now be played (experimental)

**What:** "Global" cards are effects that stay in play once cast. Until
now, playing a second copy of one you already had out was simply
refused. Now each copy stacks: both run their own effect and expire on
their own schedule. The game's own glossary now says "Copies stack
(experimental)" wherever that card type is explained.

**Why:** T asked for this change and marked it experimental on purpose —
it's a live rule change the team wants to watch in play before deciding
whether it's the final rule.

**Commits:** `4d8ac37a` (phase GS1)

**No capture:** a rules change with no new screen; the in-game glossary
text is the only visible trace, and it is quoted above in full.

## [mechanics] A refused "pay with life" now points at exactly why

**What:** Some cards let you pay their cost in your own life total
(VITAE) instead of a die. The game already refused a payment that would
have dropped you below 1 life — but it just silently did nothing. Now a
refusal like that flashes the life-total ring red, shows a ghost segment
for the amount you'd have lost, and tags the exact cost you couldn't
afford.

**Why:** part of the same card-cost work as the item above; a refusal
with no visible reason reads as a bug even when it's the game working as
designed. Phase SF1 closes that gap.

**Commits:** `f8bf4fc5` (phase SF1)

**No capture:** a 900ms animated callout on the life-total ring; the
automated capture set does not include combat mid-animation states.

## [content] A dead card gets a second use

**What:** One of the Blood Price trial cards, P-04c, used to do nothing
at all unless you were already at half life or below — a card that sat
dead in your hand most of a fight. It's now always useful: free, it
heals you a little; paid, it costs you some life to heal back double
whatever you lost that round.

**Why:** T asked for this directly, in an attended design session — the
card's old wording made it dead weight for most of a fight, which is a
worse feeling than a weak card.

**Commits:** `f59eadf1`

**Evidence:** card bp-p-04c — reprinted (see the before/after card face
below)

## [mechanics] Two small combat-fairness bugs fixed

**What:** Two bugs from the same bug hunt got fixed together. First, a
die could get "stuck" on a card that couldn't actually be played right
then, wasting your turn's resource on nothing. Second, one card (P-07b)
was supposed to heal you for exactly the life you'd just lost, but a
scaling rule meant to apply elsewhere was quietly inflating that number
— so its printed healing cap wasn't being respected.

**Why:** both were found during a full manual walk of the Act 1 content
(the "RC2 walk") on 2026-10-11, and both are cases where the number or
outcome on screen didn't match what the card actually promised.

**Commits:** `8efb0375` (die refusal), `0cd35985` (P-07b heal math)

**No capture:** both are rules-engine corrections with no new screen;
the fix is in what number lands, not in anything drawn differently.

## [ui] A round of fresh-eyes fixes

**What:** A handful of smaller things the game's own automated
playtesting caught, all fixed this window: shop prices in the village
and the blacksmith were printing a shorthand ("12s") while every other
screen spells out "SHILLINGS" — now they all match. A sealed-off spot on
the map was drawing so faint it looked like it had vanished rather than
just being locked. And three different animations — the dice settling at
the start of a round, the round-counter stamp, and the title screen's
EMBARK button — could all still be tapped or pressed while still
mid-motion, which meant an early tap sometimes skipped or missed what it
was aimed at. All three now wait for their own animation to actually
finish before accepting input.

**Why:** every one of these came from the project's own unattended
fresh-eyes testing pass, which plays the game cold the way a first-time
visitor would and flags anything that reads wrong.

**Commits:** `bc12fca2`, `ef8c53c8` (shop prices), `cf095d5a` (sealed map
mark), `672b38d6` (round-start dice), `784d6a1e` (round stamp), `31f5faa4`
(EMBARK), `8ecc2871` (the dock's phase record now reads aloud as one
line for screen readers)

**No capture:** six small fixes across several screens; none changes a
screen's layout enough to warrant its own picture.

## [infra] Three self-checking tools, mentioned two entries ago, are now live and reporting

**What:** Last entry described three new internal tools, all still
waiting on review. All three are now merged and have run for real:

- The fight-difficulty measuring tool ran its first official pass: across
  every Act 1 boss door, the newer "Blood Price" card deck wins 100% of
  the time at every character build tested, while the plain starting deck
  wins as little as 20% of the time at the hardest-hit builds. No change
  was made from this — it's a reading, not a verdict (that's a design
  call for later) — but it's now a standing measurement the team can
  re-check after any change.
- The unreachable-content checker's finding stands: a ring called the
  Bleeding Signet exists in the game's files but nothing currently gives
  it to a player.
- The card rulebook checker's findings stand too: 18 existing cards
  already break their own rarity's complexity budget, and 5 keywords need
  a ruling on what mechanic should back them. Both are filed for the next
  card-design session, not fixed automatically.

**Why:** these tools exist so the team isn't guessing about fight
difficulty or quietly-broken content; their first real runs are what
they were built to produce.

**Commits:** merges `93f05dfe` / `3f9b44e5` / `be6e827d` (PRs #505, #507,
#509)

**No capture:** internal tooling and a data table; nothing changed on
any screen a player sees.
