# 2026-10-17 — A full Act 1 run now ends at level 20

> clear takes a pilgrim all the way to level 20, live combat moved off
> the exploration map and onto its own full-screen route, and a brand
> new puzzle mechanic for hazard encounters took its first steps behind
> a developer-only preview. Alongside that, several small fixes queued
> from the last entry landed, and the loop ran its first self-grading
> pass on how well its own checks actually catch mistakes.

## [balance] Leveling now runs the full length of Act 1, to level 20

**What:** The experience curve was retargeted so a complete Act 1 clear
ends at character level 20 instead of wherever it landed before. Each
level costs 10% more XP than the last up to level 20 (15% dearer after
that, to 40, then 20%), and every level grants one stat point. Leveling
up no longer tops off your life total (VITAE) for free. Existing saves
keep whatever level they're already at under the new math.

**Why:** the old curve topped out short of where a full playthrough
should land a character; this retargets the whole arc so Act 1 has room
to use all 20 levels the game's own stat-point math expects.

**Commits:** `6d7cf28f` (phase LV1)

**No capture:** a numbers change to the XP table and stat-point grant;
no screen changed shape because of it.

## [infra] Live combat moves onto its own screen; the old overlay plumbing is gone

**What:** Combat used to render as a modal layered on top of the
exploration map the whole time a fight was on screen. It now gets its
own dedicated full-screen route, the same way the hazard and rest
screens already do — no back button or swipe-to-dismiss while a fight
is in progress. The older system that used to host combat as an overlay
(its chain bars, its tab-bar locking, and the sandbox screen it was
first built in) has been deleted outright, along with a new automated
test that plays a fight in the Labyrinth start to finish to prove the
new route holds up.

**Why:** the overlay approach was legacy plumbing from before the
game's last major rebuild; moving combat to its own route removes an
entire layer of indirection that every future combat change would
otherwise have had to work around.

**Commits:** `0e4c6432` (phase CR1a), `118216b6` (phase CR1b)

**No capture:** a fight still looks and plays the same; what changed is
how the screen is hosted, which has no visual trace of its own.

## [mechanics] A new hazard puzzle takes its first steps, behind a developer-only preview

**What:** A new mechanic for hazard encounters is taking shape: a fixed
puzzle layout (a starting hand, a predetermined reserve, dice, a goal
per route and a difficulty band) played once with no randomness, with
an internal "solver" that proves each layout is actually winnable and
sits in its intended difficulty band. Clearing one now has real stakes:
XP, a loot roll, a full-completion bonus, and scaled life loss (with a
lasting "scar") on a weak finish. Optional thresholds can unlock an
extra die, an extra card, or turn blank cards into authored ones. Three
hand-built example puzzles (easy, medium, hard) are playable right now
from a HAZARD PUZZLES board in the developer menu, each against its own
countdown clock.

**Why:** this is new content under active construction, authored and
checked piece by piece; it is not yet reachable from a normal playthrough
and is being proven out in the developer menu first.

`37e3712a` (phases B3a–B3e)

## [ui] The combat screen's phase clock gets easier to read

**What:** The small readout at the top of the fight screen that tracks
which phase, round and turn you're on used to abbreviate "ROUND" and
"TURN" down to single letters at a tiny size. It now spells out
"PHASE · ROUND · TURN" in full, at a larger size.

**Why:** this was a fresh-eyes finding from the project's own unattended
testing pass (critique pass 88) — a readout a player actually needs
mid-fight was too small and too cryptic to read at a glance.

**Commits:** `0dc39188`

**Shot:** combat-encounter — before/after/diff of the fight screen's
clock readout.

## [ui] A refused "pay with life" is now announced out loud, not just shown

**What:** Some cards let you pay their cost with your own life total
instead of a die. When the game refuses a payment that would have
dropped you below 1 life, it already flashed the life-total ring red —
now it also announces that refusal to a screen reader, the same as
every other refusal in combat.

**Why:** a visual-only refusal reads as nothing happening at all to
anyone using a screen reader; this closes that gap for players who rely
on one.

**Commits:** `6974aa46`

**No capture:** an accessibility announcement with no visible on-screen
change of its own.

## [mechanics] A hazard scar now mends at any rest, not only at an inn

**What:** A lingering injury ("scar") picked up from a bad hazard outcome
used to only heal when resting at an inn specifically. It now mends at
any rest — inn, camp, or otherwise.

**Why:** a full manual playthrough of Act 1 (the "RC2 walk") flagged that
the scar's own in-game wording never actually promised "only at an inn";
the fix brings the behavior in line with what the game already tells the
player.

**Commits:** `3d953081`

**No capture:** a rules-engine correction with no new screen.

## [infra] Sentry learns to read performance and crashes on real devices

**What:** Preview builds (the test APKs built ahead of a real release)
now report slow or frozen frames, app startup time, and a timing span
for every card drag in combat, to the project's error-tracking service.
Production and web builds send none of this. Crash reports from those
same preview builds now also name which build they came from and
decode their own stack traces automatically instead of showing raw,
unreadable addresses.

**Why:** this gives the team real performance and crash data from
device-like builds ahead of time, rather than finding out about a slow
screen or an unreadable crash report after a release.

**Commits:** `3686d84d` (phase PF3), `f91ed7a0`

**No capture:** backend telemetry wiring with no player-visible screen.

## [infra] The loop runs its first self-grading pass

**What:** The project's automated build-and-ship loop built a set of
tools to check how well its own safety nets actually work. One tool
tabulates every automated pull request merged in the last several days
against which checks ran and which were skipped. A second replays each
of those pull requests' new tests against the code as it stood *before*
the fix, to catch any test that would have passed even without the fix
it was supposed to prove (4 of 58 test files currently would have). A
third deliberately plants small bugs in the core fight-resolution code
and measures how often the existing automated tests actually catch them
(76.3% of the time, right now). A fourth ranks which fixes would have
caught the most for the least effort, and sets concrete targets for the
next round.

**Why:** the team wants to know whether its own quality gates are
actually catching mistakes before shipping, rather than assuming they
are; this is the first real measurement of that.

**Commits:** `86eea99d`, `3c0dc312`, `15a2fa76`, `dd6f39c1`, `f51fa926`
(phases EV1a–EV1d)

**No capture:** internal process tooling and a set of numbers; nothing
changed on any screen a player sees.
