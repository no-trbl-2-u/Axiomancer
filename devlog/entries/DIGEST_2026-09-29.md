# 2026-09-29 — The whole game resets to a tiny, honest core

> Two digests ago, a routine cleanup pass turned up something the designer
> couldn't unsee: raising your character's stats had never once changed
> how hard your cards hit. Rather than patch that one broken wire, he
> called a planning session and made a bigger call — strip almost
> everything down to what's proven to work, and rebuild it deliberately,
> with him in the room. That call is now underway. Stats drive your
> damage for real. Alignment and the GRACE meter are gone. The card shelf
> has dropped from well over a hundred cards to three. The monster roster
> has dropped from seventy-nine to three. Two dead-end shortcuts in the
> world now say so instead of pretending to open. None of this is loss —
> it's the game clearing its own workbench before building again.

## [mechanics] Character stats finally drive combat, and alignment/GRACE are gone

**What:** A card's DEAL, GUARD and similar keyword numbers now scale off
the stat they're tied to — `base × stat ÷ 5` — instead of ignoring your
Body, Mind and Heart investment entirely. Health also moved onto a real
formula (50 base, plus a chunk per stat). Alongside that, the entire
alignment system is gone: the three-axis philosophy grid, its 27 cells,
and the GRACE meter (the HUD track, the memoir band, the mercy bonus it
used to add, and the cost it used to attach to withdrawing from a fight)
have all been deleted. Dialogue choices that used to be hidden behind an
alignment or morality threshold aren't gone — they're just no longer
gated, so every player now sees them. A card's colour label changed name
internally (`philosophicalAspect` → `color`) with no change to how it
plays.

**Why:** The previous digest reported the discovery that a character's
stats never touched combat math — this window ships the fix. The
designer's stat-model decisions folded the alignment and GRACE removal in
alongside it, judging that a 27-cell philosophy grid and a separate mercy
meter were complexity the new, stat-driven model didn't need — and that
gating story choices behind invisible alignment thresholds was hiding
content from players rather than adding to it.

**Commits:** `53b6a05b` (stat scaling, S3), `67fd0106` (alignment/GRACE
removal, T6)

**No capture:** both changes are engine and data surgery; the alignment
grid and GRACE HUD track had no baseline screenshot capturing them
specifically, so their removal has no before/after picture this window.

## [content] The card shelf purges down to three "grey" basics

**What:** The player's card library is now three cards: A Plain Blow (a
plain attack), A Plain Ward (a plain block) and A Plain Word (which
applies VULNERABLE, now with no upper cap). Everything else — 132 named
cards, the preset decks, the "haunt"/"ally"/"curse" card families, and
the system that injected curses into a run — is gone. Post-fight card
rewards and loot caches now only ever offer from that same three-card
pool. The keyword glossary lost three more entries (PIERCE, RIPOSTE,
FORETELL) that no longer described anything any card or monster actually
did.

**Why:** With stats now driving real damage, every one of those 132 cards
would have needed individual re-tuning to make sense under the new math —
work the designer judged not worth doing twice. Emptying the shelf to
three basics and rebuilding from there, card by card, in sessions with
him, was ruled the faster and more honest path (this is the same "card
hold" the last digest described, now acted on).

**Commits:** `73c408e7` (the purge, P1), `e663ef05` (keyword/glyph
carrier sweep), `85b163be` (PIERCE/RIPOSTE/FORETELL removed)

**Evidence:** card grey-word — new

## [mechanics] THE REVAMP: the designer commits to rebuilding from a tiny, honest core

**What:** In a planning session, the designer ratified a full "reset to
core" plan for the whole game, not just its cards. The surviving core:
three player cards, three monsters, one four-region road (Act One only),
eleven relics (each now a placeholder), healing potions as the only item,
and a minimal hazard deck. Nearly everything past that line — the other
six maps, the other 76 monsters, most items, most of the deck — gets
deleted or set aside, piece by piece, over a numbered sequence of
"reset" steps. Once the reset finishes and a checkpoint release ships,
a second track of guided sessions with the designer rebuilds relics,
cards, monster tiers, hazards and more on top of that clean base. Until
that checkpoint, no new card, monster, relic, or story content ships from
anyone but the designer himself.

**Why:** The stat-damage finding didn't just break one number — it
exposed how much of the existing game (a 79-monster roster with eleven
overlapping keyword systems, hundreds of cards, several unfinished
continents) had been built without that foundation actually working.
Patching around it piece by piece risked repeating the same mistake
region by region. Resetting to what's proven, then rebuilding
deliberately, was the designer's own call, recorded across a run of
numbered decisions during the session.

**Commits:** `694e0f26`, `7b43b720`, `46c76ed4`

**No capture:** this is a planning decision, not a code or content
change — nothing in the game looks different because of it directly; the
following two cards are its first visible results.

## [infra] The loop starts executing the reset: doctrine cleanup, old tools retired

**What:** The loop's own tooling was reset first, before touching game
content. Every automated command now refuses to create a new card,
monster, or story beat while the reset is underway, and the commands that
used to route work to a team of content-writing assistants no longer do
— that team was retired for the duration. The in-app card-editor tool
was deleted, along with the automated balance-measurement system (the
"deck matrix") that used to grade every card change against a simulated
opponent gauntlet — neither applies to a three-card, three-monster game,
and both will return, redesigned, once the rebuild reaches that point.

**Why:** Every later reset step depends on the loop no longer trying to
add content mid-reset, and on old tools not printing stale numbers (a
three-card deck being graded by a matrix built for a hundred-plus-card
one) that would just confuse the next reading of the game's state.

**Commits:** `ba658cba` (loop doctrine reset, R0), `0cfea70c` (tooling
reset, R1)

**No capture:** internal tooling and process, no player-facing screen.

## [content] The monster roster drops from seventy-nine to three

**What:** Every monster in the game except three is gone: a common
enemy (the Float-Eye), a tougher mid-region fight (the Brine Hag), and a
boss that now guards the exit of every region (the Doorwarden). All
eleven "monster keyword" systems that used to ride along with the
retired 76 — things like a poison-style bleed tied to a curse card that
no longer exists, a self-healing loop, and a boss that could summon
reinforcements mid-fight — are deleted with them, not just unused. The
three survivors deal plain damage with no special tricks for now; those
tricks come back, redesigned, in a later guided session.

**Why:** The 76 retired monsters, and the keyword machinery built for
them, were the single largest piece of "game that doesn't rest on the new
stat foundation." Clearing them first lets every later reset step (the
world, the relics, the engine) re-point at a settled, tiny roster instead
of guessing which of 79 might still be relevant.

**Commits:** `c20ebe14` (roster + data, R2a), `872de5a7` (keyword code
deletion, R2b)

**No capture:** the nightly evidence tool tracks retired *cards*
automatically but not retired monsters, so there's no auto-generated
before/after plate for this one; the seventy-six names aren't printed
here either — git history is the record, per the reset's own stated
policy.

## [content] Two shortcuts seal shut, six old maps go quiet

**What:** The stairway at the bottom of the Lantern Deep and the vault
door into the Labyrinth — both reachable as of the last digest — are
now sealed: walking up to either shows a short scene explaining it
doesn't open, instead of leading anywhere. Six older maps (the northern
forest, the caverns, the northern city, the river crossing, the town
across the river, and the capital) are parked: their code still exists,
but no door from Act One leads to them anymore, and they spawn no
fights. A new run only ever sees the four Act One regions now.

**Why:** Those two shortcuts and the six older maps were built for a
much larger monster roster and card pool that no longer exists. Rather
than leave a door that leads to an empty, undertuned map, the reset
closes it with an honest "not yet" until the world beyond Act One can be
rebuilt around the surviving three monsters.

**Commits:** `f47fb4c9` (world reset, R3a)

**No capture:** no map art changed — this is a door behavior and a line
of cutscene text, not a new picture; the nightly plate-evidence tool
found nothing to capture.
