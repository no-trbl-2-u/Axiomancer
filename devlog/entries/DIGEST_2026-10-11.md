# 2026-10-11 — A set of cards that cost you blood

> The headline is a card session: a 30-card trial set built around paying
> your own health for power went live, got played against a real fight,
> and was revised the same day — five cards swapped, a whole card *type*
> cut, two new worn trinkets added. The boss it was tested against got a
> slower ramp to match, built on a new tool that measures a fight the way
> a player actually experiences it, not the way the simulator used to. A
> weekly speed check shipped and immediately caught three real slowdowns.
> Two bugs that only automation could trip were fixed the same day they
> were found: a scrambled die roll that had been dealing every seeded
> shuffle's first card last, and a map test chasing its own one-tick race
> after a defeat restart. And the pre-release walkthrough from ten days

> title screen's king to a card name breaking mid-word, are fixed.

## [content] The Blood Price trial set goes live — and gets revised the same day

**What:** A guided card session with the game's designer put a 30-card
trial set live: cards that pay a cost in your own health (SACRIFICE),
a foe-worn mark that slowly heals whoever placed it (LEECH), and a tether
that lets a later card reach back and land on the foe (TETHER). A test
build in the dev tools let the set be dropped into any fight without
touching real saves. A few hours after the first live fight against it,
the designer cut one whole card type (a "Curse" family) and five cards
with it, added five new cost-in-blood cards in their place (Bloodletting,
Open Vein, Blood for Blood, Crimson Pact, Transfusion), rebalanced five
more, and changed how the health-drain mark decays over a fight instead of
resetting it every turn. Two new rings went with the revision — one adds
health, the other lets your next blood-cost card hit twice for one cost.

**Why:** the first live fight against the set found real problems a
desk read couldn't: a finishing hit was healing the attacker for more
than the foe actually had left to lose, a card's own blood cost printed
on its face as if it were a gain instead of a cost, and the health-drain
mark's math didn't match how a multi-turn fight actually plays. The
designer fixed all three before the session ended rather than carrying
them to the next one.

## [balance] A boss gets a slower ramp, measured against the new cards

**What:** The Doorwarden boss's health rose and its attacks now escalate
over three stages instead of two, opening its second stage on round 4 and
its third on round 7 rather than hitting full strength almost immediately.
A new tool plays a full hand of cards against a boss the way a careful
player actually would — never spending a blood cost into a hit that would
down you, holding back a card that isn't worth it yet — and reports how
many turns a fight actually takes and how often it's won.

**Why:** the fight-length numbers the simulator had been reporting for
the game's toughest fights didn't match what the designer saw playing
live — the simulator had been testing a boss at half the health a real
fight gives it, and with a different deck than a player would actually
carry. Built correctly, the same boss now measures at six-and-a-half to
seven-and-a-half turns for most players, winning roughly two fights out
of three to nine out of ten depending on level — the seven-turn boss
fight the designer was aiming for.

**Commits:** `01133bd5` (the measuring tool), `bc483b2b` (the Doorwarden's
new numbers)

**Evidence:** foe enemy-the-doorwarden — reprinted

## [mechanics] Two old rules get corrected: a hidden bonus, and a status cap

**What:** A die of a card's own color used to quietly add 25% (or more)
to that card's numbers and extra turns to its effects — a bonus a player
had no way to see on the card itself. It's gone; a card now does exactly
what it prints, scaled only by your stats. Separately, a status like
VULNERABLE could be pushed above 30 by a card's own printed number, but a
small bonus from powering a card with extra dice was capping it back down
to 30 regardless — that cap is gone too.

**Why:** the color-match bonus was a holdover the revamp was supposed to
retire along with the rest of the old combat system, ruled out once
someone asked why a card's printed number wasn't the number it actually
dealt. The VULNERABLE cap was a genuine bug: a status was always meant to
land at whatever a card prints, uncapped, and a left-over clamp from
before that rule was quietly undoing a fifth of a big hit's worth of
extra vulnerability.

## [mechanics] A scrambled die roll — every seeded shuffle dealt the same card last

**What:** The game's random-number generator starts from a numeric seed,
and for a long run of small seeds (1 through roughly 200), the very first
number it produced was so close to zero that every one of those seeds
shuffled a deck the same way — the first card in a 30-card list always
ended up dealt last. The seed now gets scrambled before it starts
producing numbers; a saved game still resumes its exact sequence.

**Why:** every balance simulation, every scripted playtest run, and any
other seeded shuffle in the project had been quietly testing one shuffle
pattern over and over instead of two hundred different ones, without
anyone able to see it from the results alone — the numbers looked
plausible either way.

**Commits:** `fcac2db2`

**No capture:** a random-number generator fix; no screen or card changed.

## [infra] A weekly speed check ships, and catches three real slowdowns

**What:** A new Sunday job times a seeded fight, measures how much code
the app downloads and how long the first screen takes to appear, and
records three rounds of a real combat screen's smoothness in a browser —
then compares this week's numbers against last week's and a fixed budget.
Its first real run (its dry run the week before had found nothing to
compare against yet) found the app now takes slightly longer than budgeted
to show its first screen, to paint its largest piece of content, and to
let the page respond after a busy stretch — each by a small, single-digit
percentage.

**Why:** nothing had been watching the game's loading or in-fight
smoothness on a schedule before; a slow creep in either would only have
surfaced once a player noticed. Three small misses on the first real
measurement is itself unremarkable — the tool working as intended is the
headline — and each miss is now a filed, scored finding rather than an
unnoticed drift.

**Commits:** `ca2e13db` (the tool, phase PF1), `2fe31b97` (first real
reading: clean), `593dbf28` (second reading: the three misses)

**No capture:** a measurement tool; it changed no screen or card.

## [infra] Two bugs only automation could trip, both fixed the same day

**What:** The loop's own daily activity record had a gap: the line
marking that a given automated tick finished cleanly was being written
*after* that tick's last save, so on the kind of runner the loop uses,
nothing ever committed it — every day's record was quietly missing its
own closing lines. That's fixed. Separately, today's scheduled full-suite
test run failed on a map-walking check: after a defeat sends the player
back to the start of a region, a screen transition that's deliberately
delayed by a fraction of a second could — in rare timing — let the test's
very next click race against that transition and misfire.

**Why:** the telemetry gap meant the loop's own activity ledger had never
actually been complete for any automated run, despite being documented as
reliable — a tooling debt filed and fixed the same window it was found.
The map-walk race was a test-timing bug, not a player-facing one: the
screen's own delay is deliberate and correct, so the test's check for
"has the screen settled" now waits a beat longer before trusting what it
sees, rather than changing how the game itself behaves.

**Commits:** `53a0c39b` (the telemetry fix), `d02b2962` (the map-walk test
fix)

**No capture:** both fixes are to the loop's own tooling and its tests;
neither touches a screen a player sees.

## [ui] Two more screens learn the "one column" fix

**What:** On a wide desktop window, a five-card hand used to overlap its
cards so tightly that each one's cost column got covered by its neighbor,
and the hazard screen's two route cards used to stretch edge to edge with
huge gaps between a number and the label it belonged to. Both now hold to
the same capped, centered column the main menu and combat preview already
use; a phone screen is unchanged either way.

**Why:** two critique passes in a row (73) caught these on two different
screens — the fourth and fifth screens this exact bug has shown up on in
two weeks. The dispatcher has already flagged the pattern itself as a
candidate for one shared fix and one shared test instead of a fix per
screen (see *Tuning proposals* — it's still open).

**Commits:** `cc556113` (the hand), `3c25eb28` (the hazard route screen),
`8d579f67` (a card's own paid column, same root cause)

**No capture:** none of these changed a committed baseline screenshot
this window; each carries its own layout test instead.

## [ui] The pre-release walkthrough's last few items close out

**What:** Six small items left over from the "RC walk" done before the
game's first tagged release are fixed: the title screen's bottom shadow
used to be three flat bands whose edges cut straight across the king's
crown and beard on a wide window (it's one smooth gradient now); the map
camera could leave a black band above the map art on a phone when the
visible area sat near the top edge (it now slides to meet the frame
instead); a hazard screen's crack effect was passing a positioning value
straight to the browser in a way it silently ignored (fixed to a form
both the phone and the browser understand); a single long hazard card
name ("CLIFFRUNNER") was breaking mid-word when a neighboring card
covered part of it; the level-up screens were the last three places still
printing a level in roman numerals ("level iv") while every other screen
already uses plain numbers; and a cache and a forge in the forest region
were drawing a city street's art behind them instead of the forest's own.

**Why:** these are the last of a batch flagged by the walkthrough
conducted before `v0.1.0`'s release, now fully closed out — a player
opening any of these six screens no longer sees the mismatch a tester
already caught ten days ago.
