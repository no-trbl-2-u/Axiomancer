# Axiomancer Narrative Style Constitution

This is the governing document for shipped narrative copy. It converts broad
literary observations into Axiomancer-specific craft rules; it is not a license
to reproduce any author's voice. When this document conflicts with a rule or
interface, **mechanical and UI clarity wins**.

## The narrative promise

Axiomancer tells of people whose explanations alter what they can do, what they
notice, and what they permit. The world does not announce a moral verdict. It
keeps receipts: a repaired net, an unopened gate, a faction that remembers, a
status that persists. Philosophy is therefore visible as consequence rather
than lecture.

The signature movement is **claim → object → consequence**:

1. someone names a belief, rule, debt, or exception;
2. a concrete thing tests it;
3. play records the result in body, inventory, route, relationship, or world
   state.

## Seven principles

1. **Material consequence.** Attach every important abstraction to labor,
   supply, damage, access, custody, or care. “Duty” becomes who carries the wet
   rope. “Mercy” becomes a costly opening and a later witness.
2. **Landscape agency without personification.** Terrain routes action through
   slope, tide, roots, visibility, decay, and old construction. It does not
   conveniently feel the protagonist's mood.
3. **Historical scale through layers.** Show time in patched masonry, reused
   tools, blocked roads, altered rituals, and incompatible records. Do not
   inflate every scene to cosmic importance.
4. **Moral ambiguity through events.** Give each choice a beneficiary, cost,
   uncertainty, and residue. Do not append a narrator's verdict.
5. **Restrained interiority.** Prefer an action, hesitation, correction, or
   sensory fixation to a paragraph explaining emotion. Interior statements are
   brief and specific when a decision requires them.
6. **Concrete objects.** Build scenes around handled things: tally slats, fish
   hooks, resin wedges, ferry pins, cracked lenses, waxed cord, gate keys. An
   object must bear use, provenance, or choice—not merely atmosphere.
7. **Selective ritual repetition.** Repeat an exact short structure only when
   play has changed its meaning: three watches, recurring ceremonies, a vow
   answered under new conditions. Never use repetition as ambient grandeur.

## Axiomancer's original language architecture

### Cosmology

The world is understood through **claims and seams**. A claim is any rule a
person or institution asks reality to honor. A seam is where two claims meet:
forest and road, village need and royal levy, mercy and advantage. No narrator
confirms that this model is metaphysically true. Different cultures explain the
same effects differently, and mechanics decide what actually happens.

### Imagery families

Use a small, rotating set; do not stack families in one sentence.

- **Coast:** knots, tide marks, salt bloom, sounding lines, tarred seams.
- **Forest:** cambium, coppice cuts, resin, root-lifted stones, fungal shelves.
- **Labyrinth/city:** hinge wear, survey marks, patched courses, queue tokens,
  sealed apertures.
- **Reason made material:** balances, joins, counters, margins, erased tallies,
  fitted pieces. Avoid turning every thought into a book or equation.
- **Human continuity:** repairs, inherited tools, revised names, uneven wear,
  meals apportioned.

### Sentence patterns

Axiomancer prose uses designed variation, not one prestige cadence.

- Default: one concrete observation, then one changed condition.
- Pressure: short clauses in chronological order; verbs before decoration.
- Reflection: one longer sentence may connect object, provenance, and present
  use, followed by a short consequence.
- Ritual: parallel clauses capped at three; repeat only load-bearing words.
- Dialogue: pattern belongs to the speaker, never to the whole setting.

Fragments are reserved for labels, immediate perception, and aftermath beats.
Semicolons are rare. Exclamation marks belong to characters who actually use
them.

## Surface contracts

| Surface | Primary job | Style limit |
|---|---|---|
| World narration | Locate, orient, reveal changed conditions | 2–4 sensory facts; one implication at most |
| Combat | Make intent, timing, status, and outcome readable | mechanical noun and number cannot be obscured |
| Hazard/Event encounter | Present situation, stakes, choices, residue | consequence preview adjacent to each choice |
| Item description | Identify object, use, and one provenance trace | no miniature epic; mechanics in a separate line |
| Character dialogue | Express a person's goal, knowledge, and verbal habit | no shared “house voice”; information survives flavor |
| UI/mechanical copy | Enable a correct decision immediately | canonical terms, active verbs, numbers, timing |
| Lore/codex | Preserve competing records and useful context | label source/uncertainty; distinguish event from account |

## Canon and precedence

- Use **VITAE**, never a generic substitute, on player-facing combat surfaces.
- Use **STANCE** and **CHOOSE A STANCE**, never a defensive synonym that changes
  the concept.
- Status effects remain the center of combat explanation and texture.
- If atmosphere competes with target, cost, duration, timing, route, or result,
  cut atmosphere.
- Narrative may conceal motive or history; it may not conceal a rule the player
  needs for the current decision.

## Drafting test

For each paragraph ask: What changed? What object carries the change? What can
the player decide or infer? If all three answers are “nothing,” cut it.
