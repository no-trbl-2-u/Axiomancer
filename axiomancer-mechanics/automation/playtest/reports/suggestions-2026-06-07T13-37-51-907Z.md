## Propose-only (needs human judgement)

The dominant failure mode is **structural, not a global-constant miss**, and none of it is reachable by a registry tunable. The chosen design direction (owner, this tick) is: **lean on stronger skills/effects rather than expanding the round cap — keep combat streamlined.** The findings below are reframed around that direction and around the corrected root cause for the late game.

### The unifying lever — make skills and effects stronger

A global skill/effect buff is the single player-side lever that addresses three of the four clusters at once:

- **Q2 (L1 over-tuned):** stronger early skills give the level-1 player a way to fight back before dying in ~5 rounds.
- **Q4 (strategist underperforms):** the doctrine's witness already lands the most status play (20.7 skill uses/run) but can't *convert* it; stronger effects make that play decisive.
- **Timeouts (streamlined, not a bigger counter):** "resolution" = victory **or** friendship; a timeout is *neither* (`playtest.runner.ts:401`). At `l50-mixed-normal` the player does **815:1** damage and still times out, because effect-spam converges to no win condition. Rather than raise `maxRounds`, make **effects contribute to resolution** — a saturated/debuffed enemy yields (befriend), or DoTs erode HP toward victory. Same streamlined outcome, no expanded round cap. (This supersedes the earlier "soft-win / expand the round cap" proposal, which the owner declined.)

`combat.skillStatMultiplier` is the registry proxy for this, but the A/B showed a single ±25% bump (0.5→0.6) is too diffuse to win alone — the real change is structural (effect intensity/duration base values, effect→resolution wiring), which the loop can fine-tune afterward.

### Q3 — late-game trivialization: corrected root cause

**It is NOT the scaling slope.** Player and enemy stat budgets are *both linear* in level (player `level × 5`, enemy `level × ENEMY_STAT_PER_LEVEL=3`, `game-mechanics.constants.ts:108-117`), so a same-level enemy is a **flat ~60% of the player's stat weight at every level** — confirmed by HP (L50 enemy 750 vs player 1250 ≈ 60%). The budget ratio does not diverge.

What diverges is the player's **off-budget power that enemies have none of:**
- **Gear tier** — L1 Iron Blade / Hide Vest → L50 full legendary (Axiom Greatsword, Aegis Plate…); a damage+defense multiplier stacked on top of the budget that grows with progression. Enemies carry no gear.
- **Skill library** — 10 skills at L1 → 50 at L50; more effects, synergies, resolution tools.

This explains the whole matrix shape: at L1 the player has *nothing extra* (brutal), at L50 *everything extra* (trivial). It also means **the skill/effect buff above widens exactly this gap** — so Q3's fix must be its counterweight.

**Proposal (recommended):** give enemies an **off-budget "equipment tier" bonus that scales with level/region**, weighted toward **HP + defenses/resists** — the stats the now-stronger skills/effects must chew through. Level-shaped: ≈0 at L1 (so L1 stays eased by the skill buff), large at L50 (closes trivialization). A new constant/tunable (e.g. `enemy.gearTierPerLevel` or a banded table), distinct from the single global `enemy.statPerLevel` slope (which can't bend without re-breaking L1); the loop can then A/B its magnitude within a registry range. Synergy with the doctrine: higher late-game durability gives status play *room to breathe* — several rounds of meaningful effect-stacking instead of trivial-kill or pointless-timeout.

### Q2 — also has a per-enemy outlier component

Independent of the global skill buff: `reef-barnacle-colony` (17% resolution, 55% defeat across 4 cells) and `tolltaker-of-the-ford` (L1 mixed-hard 91% defeat) blow up the L1 row while same-tier `petty-cutpurse`/`thicket-ambusher`/`rimeclaw-prowler` resolve ~95–100%. A global `enemy.statPerLevel` cut would trivialize the already-fine L1 enemies to fix two outliers. **Proposal:** rebalance those two stat blocks / effect kits directly, or add a per-enemy difficulty floor. Evidence: `l1-defensive-hard` 94% defeat in 5.7 rounds with only 1.5 skill uses/run — the player dies before any status play develops (also the worst-engagement cell in the matrix).
