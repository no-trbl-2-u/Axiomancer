Crowding is not fundamentally a typography problem. It is an information-architecture problem. Recognition, commitment, and explanation occur at different moments and deserve different surfaces.

      Critique of the original four

      Relative design judgment, not measured player data. The numbers are directional: they identify what deserves a prototype, not what has already won.

      Judgment
      1 · Flip on die
      The die visibly transforms the card. Strong confirmation, more powered space, excellent mechanical theater.
      Hides the FREE alternative, slows comparison, can become ornamental motion, risks a “text-document back.”
      Keep the FREE rank visible; use a state transformation rather than relying solely on literal 3D rotation; show both states in detail.
      9/10Prototype first
      2 · Half / half
      Static clarity. Both actions remain visible and the card can be read without interaction.
      Sacrifices recognition, emotional attachment, collection value, and visual rhythm. Turns cards into menu entries.
      Use as an accessibility or dense-information mode, not the primary face.
      5/10Useful control
      3 · No wording
      Compact, language-light, potentially fast after mastery.
      Glyphs relocate language into memory. Conditions, clocks, targets, sequence, and exceptions still require grammar.
      Use glyphs for nouns and atomic verbs; retain concise text for relationships. Never simplify mechanics merely to fit pixels.
      3/10Reject as doctrine
      4 · FREE rank
      Excellent hand scanning. FREE becomes a stable identity like suit and number. Preserves space for art and powered text.
      It lies if FREE actions contain hidden riders, conditions, multiple targets, or inconsistent clocks.
      Mechanically standardize FREE actions; include a clock/target marker where required; reveal complete truth in detail.
      9.5/10Best foundation

      The questions beneath the rectangle

      Before choosing a layout, decide what a card is promising, when that promise must be visible, and how much symbolic language the player can reasonably learn.

      Information hierarchy

      • What must be visible across all five cards simultaneously?
      • What only matters after selection?
      • What is instructional rather than decisional?
      • What is the minimum card-face promise that never lies?

      Card-language budget

      • Is every FREE action one atomic operation?
      • How many operations may powered text contain?
      • How many conditions, targets, and timing clauses fit one card?
      • Do rarity and complexity rise together?

      Dynamic truth

      • Should the face show the authored rule or the exact current result?
      • Does Guard 8 become Guard 11 when the die/read is known?
      • How are caps, immunity, color mismatch, and pending triggers forecast?
      • Can preview expose causal status consequences honestly?

      Interaction cost

      • How often may detail be required before it becomes friction?
      • Can players compare two cards without repeated closing?
      • What happens when several cards are staged?
      • Does transformation preserve the planned sequence?

      Learning and mastery

      • How many glyphs can a novice retain?
      • Do glyphs form a grammar or remain isolated symbols?
      • Will experts actually stop opening detail?
      • Can players explain a card after seeing it once?

      Accessibility and localization

      • Does the layout survive larger fonts?
      • Are glyphs distinguishable without color?
      • Does reduced motion preserve transformation meaning?
      • What does a screen reader announce at hand, staged, and detail levels?

      Aesthetic function

      • Is art decoration, recognition, narrative reward, or all three?
      • Must art remain visible during commitment?
      • Could powered state reveal transformed art rather than cover it?
      • What makes a card desirable beyond its rule?

      Terminology

      • Is “PAID” truly a cost, or is the card “POWERED” by a die?
      • Does “front/back” imply separate actions or one transformed action?
      • Can labels make the card/die relationship legible before tutorial prose?
      • Does player language match engine and interface language?

      Opening recommendation

      Prototype a hybrid rather than arguing from static mockups. The likely answer is not one face but a controlled progression through states.

      Recommended model

      Rank + Transform

      FREE becomes the card’s rank. Artwork carries recognition. The powered headline advertises possibility. A legal die transforms the face into an exact commitment preview. Detail shows both authored states and defines the language.
