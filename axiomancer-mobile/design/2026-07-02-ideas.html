Five Ways to Make Combat Ours

Ideas only — nothing here is implemented. Each one grows out of a system Axiomancer already has (hidden stances, the read, stance dice, conviction, mercy) instead of borrowing another deckbuilder's spine.

1

Called Reads — Wager on the Tell

bluff · risk/reward · uses the hidden stance

Slay-the-Spire intents are honest; ours are already half-hidden. Lean in: before you APPLY, you may call the read — declare the enemy's hidden stance from its thematic tell. Call it right and the card resolves at a critical read (amplified keyword tier, refund the die). Call it wrong and the foe punishes the overreach (it gains a token, your card resolves at disadvantage).

Say nothing and the card resolves normally — calling is always optional, always a wager.

Why it's different: combat becomes poker against a liar, not arithmetic against a billboard. The player's skill is reading prose tells, which no deckbuilder does.
2

Refutation — Win the Argument, Not the Brawl

dialectic combat · alternate win · deepens mercy

Our cards are already named like rhetoric — Slippery Slope, Achilles' Gambit, Eternal Regress. Make that literal: each foe fights for a belief, shown as 2–3 face-up axiom slates beside its VITAE. Landing the keyword that contradicts a slate cracks it; crack them all and the foe is refuted — combat ends bloodlessly with better mercy spoils than any kill.

VITAE stays as the brute-force route. Two victory grammars, one hand of cards.

Why it's different: every other deckbuilder has one verb — damage. Ours gets a second verb, persuade, that reuses the same keywords players already know.
3

A Shared Dice Economy — Deny the Foe

drafting · denial · dice as run resource

Today the enemy ignores your dice. Instead: the turn's dice roll into a shared pool between you. Whatever you leave unspent, the foe drafts at phase end — a Body die sharpens its attack, a Mind die sharpens its read of you. Suddenly "which die do I take?" is also "which die do I dare leave?"

Long game: dice can chip. A die that powers a backfired play cracks — cracked faces roll one tier lower for the rest of the run, until a rest-site mends them.

Why it's different: resource denial drafting mid-combat exists in board games (Sagrada, Grand Austria) but no roguelike deckbuilder — and it makes every turn adversarial, not solitaire.
4

The Momentum Wheel — Stance Sequencing

combo grammar · hand routing · rewards planning

Heart beats Mind beats Body beats Heart — the RPS wheel already exists. Let card order ride it: play cards whose stances step around the wheel (Heart → Body → Mind → Heart…) and each step builds one Momentum pip; pips raise the next card's keyword one tier (Bleed 18 → 24 → 30…).

Break the sequence and the pips bank into Conviction instead — never wasted, just spent differently. Suddenly your hand is a route, not a pile.

Why it's different: combo systems usually key off card types or costs; keying off the same RPS axis the enemy hides ties every mechanic in the game to one wheel.
5

The Moon Clock — a Battlefield That Acts

environment · timing layer · uses the scene art

The moonlit arena is now literally the screen — let it play too. A small moon dial in the HUD ticks each phase; every third tick lands a battlefield beat drawn from the biome: ember-rain (1 burn to both sides), ground-mist (attacks can whiff), full moonlight (all stances revealed for one phase).

Two cheap stance-flavored positioning cards — Advance / Withdraw — shift which beats reach you, so the clock is playable, not weather.

Why it's different: deckbuilder fights happen in a void. Ours happen somewhere — and the somewhere has a turn in the initiative order.
